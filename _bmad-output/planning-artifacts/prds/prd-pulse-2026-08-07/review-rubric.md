# PRD Quality Review — pulse

*Reviewed 2026-08-08 against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Scope calibration: solo personal project at a public-portfolio quality bar, chain-top (feeds architecture → UX → epics). Absence of enterprise apparatus — stakeholder sign-off, ROI, rollout, SLAs — is correct and is not penalised. The deliberate exclusion of "what any individual visual shows" (§0, §4.4 Notes) is treated as a scope decision, not an omission.*

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely good PRD. It has a real thesis — the archive/glance loop of §1, "the raw archive only compounds if pulse keeps being used, and pulse is only used if the reports are worth opening" — and the feature set, the MVP slice and the non-goals all follow from it rather than sitting beside it. The §1.3 layer boundary ("The non-negotiable is one layer: visual authoring") is the kind of decision most PRDs bury as a consideration, and it is stated as a decision with the condition under which it is wrong. FR consequences are, for the most part, genuinely testable rather than decorative.

What is at risk is downstream extraction, not judgment. Four cross-references resolve to nothing or to the wrong target — including both references to a non-existent `addendum §D`, which is the *only* content NFR-4 has. Two domain nouns that FRs depend on ("indicator", "shape") are absent from a Glossary that opens by declaring synonym introduction "a discipline violation", while one Glossary term ("Visual language") is defined and never used. And the PRD commits to duckdb-wasm on every page while carrying no latency or page-weight bound anywhere, despite UJ-1 being a five-second interaction. None of this is hard to fix; all of it will cost an architecture or epic workflow real time if it isn't.

---

## Decision-readiness — strong

The PRD makes decisions and says so. §1.3 does the hardest work in the document: it names precisely which layer is closed ("No chart or visualization library, at any level"), declares everything below it open, and pre-empts the relitigation by naming the trade — "Harder reuse is an accepted cost" (§1.2). It then states its own falsification condition: "if purpose-built visuals prove slow to produce *even with agent authoring*, the premise fails and this is the first decision to revisit." That is a PRD willing to lose.

Goal 3 (§1.1 Playground) is unusually honest: "This goal was unstated in the original pitch and surfaced only under challenge." Declaring a goal that exists for enjoyment rather than utility, and fencing it against optimisation, is the opposite of smoothing to neutral.

§10's Open Questions are actually open. OQ-1 carries three named candidates forward with a cost ledger rather than a preference; OQ-2 explicitly refuses to settle by argument — "should be settled by building one visual each way." OQ-3 says why deferral is safe rather than asserting it. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions (the deliberate refusal to specify visual content; the deferred correct-but-expensive alternative to publishing suspect data), not at safe checkpoints.

### Findings

- **medium** The addendum's sharpest self-criticism never surfaces in the PRD (§1.3 vs addendum §B.2) — addendum §B.2 states plainly that "Observable Framework plus a self-imposed 'no plotting library' discipline would deliver roughly 85% of pulse at roughly 15% of the cost" and then instructs: "The PRD should state why the remaining 15% is worth it rather than leave the question implicit — otherwise the first efficiency-minded reviewer, human or agent, reopens it." §1.3 answers *why* (goal 3, expressive layer) but never surfaces *how much is being paid*. A reader of the PRD alone cannot see the size of the bet, which is exactly the condition the addendum warns about. *Fix:* add one sentence to §1.3 carrying the 85/15 magnitude and the answer, so the trade is visible without opening the addendum.
- **low** §1.2 holds two principles in unreconciled tension — "**Predictable structure, shared implementation.** … Shared render components, macros and functions carry the implementation" against "**Structure follows content.** No premature universal abstraction, ever imposed up front." Both are right and the resolution (extract on second use, cf. FR-1's "If several sources require the same specific code, then this should become part of the shared ingestion code") exists elsewhere in the document but is not stated where the tension is. *Fix:* one clause naming the sequencing rule that reconciles them.

## Substance over theater — strong

No personas, correctly — §2 is Jobs To Be Done split across "As the user" / "As the builder", which is the right shape for a single operator who is also the author. §2.2 Non-Users is doing real work, not padding: "There is no multi-user model, no configuration UI, no onboarding path, and no support commitment" closes off a whole class of downstream inference.

The novelty claims are earned and bounded. §1.3 opens by conceding the stack is not novel ("Evidence.dev ships it; Observable Framework ships it with a stronger ingest story") before naming what is declined. Addendum §B.2's "Genuinely uncovered" list flags its own weakest item as "Uncovered, though also unproven as a differentiator." This is the rare case of a differentiation section that survives its own research.

The §1.2 principles each bind something later — "Warehouse disposable, raw durable" produces FR-6; "Annotations are content, not decoration" produces FR-16; "The data interface is the stable contract" produces FR-10. None is free-floating.

NFRs mostly avoid boilerplate: NFR-3's "The separation is structural, not procedural — it does not depend on remembering to exclude anything" is a product-specific threshold, not an adjective. NFR-5 ("pulse may break, change schema, or be rebuilt from scratch without notice") is a negative NFR, which is a good sign — the document is willing to state what it does *not* require.

### Findings

- **low** NFR-1 is a principle wearing an NFR's clothes (§6) — "Agent legibility is the ease metric" carries no bound and no failure condition; it restates §1.2 in the NFR section and claims to bind "§4.1 through §4.8 equally." It is genuinely load-bearing as a design constraint, so the fix is placement, not deletion. *Fix:* either give it one detectable consequence (e.g. every workflow is a markdown file an agent can execute without opening unrelated code — which FR-22 already almost provides), or move it to §1.2 where unbounded principles belong.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated and the document is built on it. §1's loop argument is not decoration — it produces the ordering claim "**Visualization is therefore co-equal with ingestion, not downstream of it**", which is then externally corroborated rather than asserted ("every comparable personal-data-warehouse project surveyed … converges on the same ingestion stack and then dies at the presentation layer"). Prioritisation follows: §7.1's MVP is a vertical slice ending in "One real report on French macroeconomic data, with several purpose-built visuals" rather than a horizontal ingestion-first build, which is precisely what the thesis demands. The two §7.1 promotions are each justified in-line ("*promoted from Should: FR-19 has nowhere else to live*") — de-scoping and up-scoping are both visible.

SM-1 validates the thesis rather than measuring activity, and SM-7's "Valid on its own terms, explicitly including the case where SM-1 and SM-2 disappoint" is an honest hedge rather than a hedge that erases the metric.

Where coherence thins is the metric layer's mechanics: no counter-metrics, no measurement occasion for the subjective metrics, and thirteen of twenty-four FRs unlinked to any SM.

### Findings

- **medium** No counter-metrics (§8) — the memlog records SM-C1/SM-C2 as deliberately deleted, so this is a decision, not an oversight; judged as it stands, the consequence is that §8 has no metric that can go the wrong way. Every SM improves when pulse is used more and built more. The two live tensions the PRD itself names — §9's "Portfolio pressure can suppress useful-but-messy work and personally revealing indicators", and §1.3's premise-failure condition on authoring speed — are exactly what a counter-metric would catch, and now nothing in §8 catches either. *Fix:* if counter-metrics stay out, add one sentence to §9 naming the observation that would signal each risk has materialised, so the risks remain falsifiable even without SMs.
- **medium** Thirteen FRs are validated by no SM (§8) — FR-1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16, 17, 21, 24 appear in no `Validates` list. Most are acceptable (plumbing), but **FR-10 is not**: addendum §B.2 calls the data-interface contract "not a chore but the actual contribution," and §8 has no metric that touches it. SM-4 (flat extension cost) is its natural home and cites FR-13/14/22/23 but not FR-10. *Fix:* add FR-10 (and FR-11) to SM-4's validation list.
- **medium** SM-2 and SM-3 are unmeasurable by construction (§8) — "opened as part of a rhythm rather than as a remembered task" and "pulse is where Yann goes, ahead of a search engine" have no threshold, no observation window, and no instrumentation; no FR provides any, and NFR-3's privacy stance plus static hosting make analytics unlikely to arrive. SM-1 at least states its protocol ("Crude test: state them from memory, then check"). Self-report is fine at this scale; silence about *when* and *how* is not. *Fix:* give SM-2/SM-3 the same one-line protocol treatment SM-1 has, e.g. a review occasion ("assessed at the six-month mark by recall of the last month's openings").

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR structure is better than most: every FR carries an explicit **Consequences (testable)** block, and the majority are genuinely verifiable — "No chart library appears in the dependency tree" (FR-12), "A dataset added without documentation is detectable" (FR-8), "Opening reports locally is a single command" (FR-18), "No annotation is expressed as a hardcoded coordinate" (FR-16), "A public build contains no private report and no data belonging to one" (FR-17). There is almost no adjective-driven requirement text; I found no "gracefully", no "user-friendly", no "reasonable performance". FR-19's four consequences are each individually checkable, which matters because FR-19 is the safety net for the failure mode §4.7 defines so well ("The dangerous failure is the one that succeeds").

The gaps are specific and concentrated. FR-14's own statement is untestable and, after edits, is left with a single consequence that does not test it. §4.8's workflows require an extension unit the document never defines. And the entire document contains no performance bound of any kind, which is a real omission given the decisions §4.6 makes.

### Findings

- **high** "Indicator" is a required extension unit with no definition (§3, FR-22, §4.8, UJ-4, SM-1, SM-3) — FR-22's sole consequence mandates that "add an indicator" have "a workflow that can be followed without reading unrelated system code", but *indicator* is absent from the Glossary and no FR relates it to Source, Dataset, Visual or Report. Downstream, epic breakdown must produce a workflow for a unit whose boundaries it has to invent, and SM-1 ("Core indicators … recalled to the right magnitude") measures something undefined. This is the one place where the document's otherwise-strict vocabulary discipline (§3: "Introducing a synonym anywhere in this document is a discipline violation") has a hole at a load-bearing point. *Fix:* add **Indicator** to §3 and state its relation to Dataset — most plausibly a named column or measure within a dataset — or, if "add an indicator" is really "add a column to an existing dataset", say that in FR-22 and drop the term.
- **high** No performance or page-weight bound anywhere (§4.6, §6) — FR-18 commits that "Data is queried in the browser via duckdb-wasm on all pages", and addendum §C.4 confirms this was chosen over baked JSON deliberately ("uniform wasm turns out to be the simpler option"). duckdb-wasm has a multi-megabyte cold-start cost, addendum §A.3 recommends "roughly 5–50 MB per file", and addendum §A.2 accepts single-threaded EH with "roughly linear slowdown on scan/aggregate-heavy work". Meanwhile UJ-1 is a mid-conversation lookup and SM-1/SM-2 depend on pulse being pleasant enough to open habitually. The PRD has no NFR bounding time-to-first-figure, so architecture has no target to design against and no basis for choosing between candidates on this axis. *Fix:* add an NFR with a bound — e.g. time from click to first rendered figure on a cold load, and a per-report data-transfer ceiling — even if the number is a first guess, marked as revisable.
- **high** Report interactivity is never scoped in or out (§4.5, §10 OQ-3) — OQ-3 states "only cross-filtering would force the choice early", which presupposes a decision about whether reports are interactive at all. No FR in §4.5 mentions interaction, filtering, parameters or drill-down; §5 Non-Goals does not exclude them (the memlog records cross-filtering being removed from Non-Goals while the OQ-3 reference stayed). Since duckdb-wasm-on-every-page exists largely to make client-side querying possible, a downstream reader cannot tell whether interactivity is an unstated MVP expectation or a silent non-goal. *Fix:* one line — either a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` in §5 stating reports are read-only in v1, or an FR admitting a bounded interaction surface.
- **medium** FR-14 no longer tests its own claim (§4.4) — the requirement is "must not require reinventing the system", and its single surviving consequence is "Existing visuals continue to work when the system is extended for a new kind", which tests non-regression, not bounded cost. The memlog records the removal of "change is additive and bounded, not a rewrite of shared machinery". As written, an extension that rewrites the entire data interface but leaves old visuals working satisfies FR-14. It is also the FR that SM-4 (flat extension cost) leans on hardest. *Fix:* restore a bounding consequence, or fold FR-14 into FR-13 and drop the standalone requirement.
- **medium** FR-23's "shape" is undefined and doing load-bearing work (§4.8, §1.2, NFR-1, UJ-4) — "Two sources added at different times have the same shape" is presented as testable, but *shape* is not a Glossary term. §1.2 glosses it once parenthetically ("the *shape* — file layout, naming, wiring steps, conventions") and that gloss is the only definition in the document, four sections away from the FR that tests against it. The memlog also records FR-23 losing its anti-premature-abstraction consequence, leaving §1.2's "Structure follows content" with no testable expression anywhere. *Fix:* promote **Shape** to §3 with the §1.2 gloss; consider restoring a consequence that expresses the emergent-convention rule.
- **medium** Homepage navigation is in MVP scope with no requirement (§4.7, §7.1) — §3 defines Homepage as doing two jobs ("Provides navigation to reports and displays pipeline state") and §7.1 scopes in "Homepage with navigation and pipeline state", but FR-19 and its consequences cover only the state half. The only navigation-adjacent consequence is FR-19's "Reaching a report from the homepage passes the reader through this state", which assumes the navigation it never requires. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-19 requiring every published report to be reachable from the homepage — this also makes UJ-1's path testable.
- **low** FR-1's second consequence is a policy, not a test (§4.1) — "If several sources require the same specific code, then this should become part of the shared ingestion code" is a good rule (and the missing half of the §1.2 tension noted above), but it is not a condition anyone can check on a build. *Fix:* move it to the feature Description or to §1.2, leaving the Consequences block purely verifiable.
- **low** FR-6's "equivalent result" is unbounded (§4.2) — "Deleting the entire warehouse and rebuilding produces an equivalent result" leaves open whether equivalence is byte-identical, row-identical, or semantically equal. Given "warehouse disposable, raw durable" is a core principle, the strength of the guarantee is worth one word. *Fix:* say which equivalence (most likely: identical row content per dataset, ignoring file-level nondeterminism).

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 Non-Goals is doing real work and is unusually decisive: eight entries, each naming a plausible expectation and killing it, several with the reason preserved for later revival — "Because all snapshots are retained, revision archaeology remains possible later as an opt-in extra dataset per source." "A chart library, or a consumer of one. **At any layer, ever.**" is the correct register for the one non-negotiable. §7.4's "Everything in §5" closes the loop cleanly.

The MoSCoW split in §7 is honest in both directions: promotions are annotated with their reason, and the Could tier admits that the private-data path may not ship at all, which §4.1's `[ASSUMPTION]` on FR-4 flags at the point of use. §4.7's `[NOTE FOR PM]` on the deferred last-good-snapshot rebuild is the model case — it names the better alternative, admits "It is more correct", states the cost ("snapshot-selection machinery, per-source last-pass tracking and per-source rebuild for a rare event"), and records the deferral rather than quietly dropping it.

§0's scope-discipline paragraph is the strongest single move in the document: it states the exclusion, states where those decisions *do* get made ("per visual, in session, against real data"), and pre-empts the reader who assumes an omission ("Those decisions are deliberately absent here and belong nowhere in this document").

Open-items density is right for the stakes: five Open Questions, four assumptions, two PM notes on a pre-architecture PRD for a solo project — and §10 even lists a resolved item with its resolution, which is the correct way to stop a settled question reopening.

### Findings

- **low** No `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` inline callouts (§4) — §5 and §7.4 carry the load and mostly succeed, but the callout mechanism is unused at the two points where a reader is most likely to silently assume a feature: §4.5 (interactivity — see the high finding above) and §4.7 (alerting/notification, nowhere addressed; the homepage is the only channel and the PRD never says notification is out). *Fix:* two inline callouts, or two more lines in §5.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The scaffolding is deliberately built for extraction and mostly succeeds. §0 states the intent explicitly ("written for the downstream BMad workflows that consume it"). FR IDs run FR-1…FR-24 with no gaps or duplicates; UJ-1…UJ-6 and SM-1…SM-7 likewise; every UJ is realised by at least one feature and every feature block names the UJs it realises. Sections are pull-out-able — cross-references go via Glossary terms and FR IDs, not "see above". The Glossary itself is disciplined in most places: Snapshot, Dataset, Warehouse, Build profile, Assertion and Freshness are each used verbatim wherever they appear.

The damage is a cluster of broken and mispointed references, most of them traceable to the addendum being restructured after the PRD referenced it. Individually small; collectively they mean a downstream agent following citations lands on nothing or on the wrong content four times, and one of those is an NFR's entire substance.

### Findings

- **high** `addendum §D` does not exist and is cited twice, once as an NFR's only content (§6 NFR-4, §9) — the addendum has sections A, B and C only. NFR-4 reads "pulse runs within free tiers of its hosting and automation providers under normal operation. See [addendum §D](addendum.md) for the storage-quota analysis and its watch item" — the reference is the entire quantitative content of the NFR, so as it stands NFR-4 has no bound at all. §9's Storage quota risk cites the same missing section for "Analysis, decision and mitigations". The material exists at addendum **§A.1** (10 GiB storage + 10 GiB bandwidth/month, post-paid overage, `actions/checkout` defaulting to `lfs: false`). The memlog records the watch item being "dropped entirely" while these two pointers survived. *Fix:* repoint both to §A.1, and inline the quota figures into NFR-4 so it bounds something on its own.
- **medium** FR-9 points at the wrong FR (§4.3) — "A failing assertion does not stop the pipeline run — see FR-19." FR-19 is *Pipeline state on the homepage* and says nothing about continuation. The requirement being cited is **FR-21** ("A failing assertion does not stop other sources, datasets or reports from building"). A reader chasing the guarantee lands on the display requirement instead of the behaviour requirement. *Fix:* change to FR-21.
- **medium** §4.4's `[NOTE FOR PM]` cites a section that does not contain its evidence (§4.4 → addendum §C.5) — "Attempts to derive such rules from the goals were raised and rejected four times during discovery; see [addendum §C.5]." Addendum §C.5 is *Superseded terminology and framings* — a four-item list about "hand-built", baked JSON, Git LFS and the reinvention list. It contains no record of the rejections. This is the citation backing the document's largest deliberate exclusion, so it is the one a sceptical downstream reader is most likely to follow. (The memlog logs the rejection at "3rd correction of the same error", so the count in the PRD is also unverifiable from the addendum.) *Fix:* add the rejection record to addendum §C as its own subsection and repoint, or drop the citation and let §0's scope-discipline paragraph carry it alone.
- **medium** `[NOTE FOR PM]` in FR-21 points to §8 for something §8 does not contain (§4.7) — "Recorded as a future goal; see §8." §8 is *Success Metrics*; the deferred last-good-snapshot rebuild appears nowhere in it. The natural homes are §9 (Risks — where "Silent success with bad data" already lives and would be strengthened by naming the deferred mitigation) or §5. *Fix:* repoint to §9 and add the deferred alternative as a named future mitigation there.
- **medium** "Visual language" is defined in the Glossary and used nowhere (§3, FR-13, §7.2) — §3 defines it as "the store of conventions that apply across visuals and that the authoring agent consumes", then FR-13 says "Conventions that apply across visuals live in one place that the authoring agent consumes" without naming it, and §7.2 scopes in "A store for cross-visual conventions". Three names, one concept, in a document that opens §3 with "Introducing a synonym anywhere in this document is a discipline violation." Downstream, the artifact that §7.2 puts in scope has no stable name to build a story around. *Fix:* use **Visual language** verbatim in FR-13 and §7.2, or delete the Glossary entry and standardise on "cross-visual conventions".
- **low** Assumptions Index roundtrip fails in one direction (§11) — four entries are indexed, but only two have inline `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags in the body (FR-4, FR-7). The §7.1 entry ("One public source and one report constitute the v1 vertical slice") and the §2.3 entry ("User journeys are stated lightly…") have no inline anchor at their cited locations. The §7.1 one matters more: a reader of §7.1 alone sees the MVP slice as settled, not assumed. *Fix:* add the inline tags at §7.1 and §2.3, or drop the two index entries if they are now settled decisions rather than assumptions.
- **low** dbt vocabulary leaks into §7.1 ahead of the decision it presumes (§7.1 vs §4.3, §10 OQ-1) — "A transformation layer producing one wide, documented, tested dataset **from staging models**". "Staging models" is dbt-specific and appears nowhere in §3 or §4.3, which are carefully tool-agnostic ("through a declared transformation layer"). Addendum §C.4 does settle layer B as dbt-duckdb, so the fact is right; the leak is that the PRD proper never states it, so §7.1 references a concept the document has not introduced. *Fix:* either name the dbt-duckdb decision once in §4.3 (it is settled, and §7.1 already commits to Git LFS and parquet in the same spirit), or replace "staging models" with a neutral phrase.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a solo, single-operator tool held to a portfolio bar, and the PRD is calibrated to that rather than to a template: no personas, no stakeholder map, no rollout plan, no SLA — and §2.2 explicitly forecloses the multi-user reading that would have required them.

UJ density is judged correctly and the judgment is stated rather than assumed: §2.3's "Deliberately light — a single operator, no authentication, no multi-device handoff", with the reasoning carried into §11 as an indexed assumption. Six UJs is right for six genuinely distinct operating modes, and each carries its protagonist inline — five as Yann, UJ-6 as "A stranger", which is the correct protagonist for the reproducibility journey precisely *because* they are not named. The pair UJ-2/UJ-3 is a good instinct: the healthy path and the failing path are separate journeys because the failing path is the one FR-19 and FR-21 exist for.

Success Metrics are appropriately mixed — SM-1/2/3 are experiential because the thesis is about retention, SM-5/SM-6 are operational, SM-7 is explicitly craft. No DAU/MAU tell. NFRs are correspondingly scoped down, and NFR-5 ("No service continuity constraint") is the right anti-NFR for a tool nothing depends on.

It is also correctly shaped as chain-top: §0 names its consumers, the Glossary exists for them, and IDs are stable "so downstream artifacts have stable references." Which is why the downstream-usability findings above carry the severity they do — this PRD has explicitly opted into being extracted from.

No findings.
